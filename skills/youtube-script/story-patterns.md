# Story patterns for a talking-head video

Use these to judge a cut or to shape an outline. They are patterns, not a formula: a video that follows one exactly can still be dull, and a good one may bend all of them.

## The shape
- **Question and answer.** Every video answers one question the viewer has (or should have after the first thirty seconds). Name it in one sentence. The answer arrives late enough to be earned and early enough that nobody leaves first.
- **Result first, then the road.** Showing the finished thing, the failure or the surprising number before explaining how it came about gives the viewer a reason to stay for the explanation.
- **The setback.** Something goes wrong or turns out harder than expected, and the host reacts honestly. A video with no setback reads as a brochure.
- **The payoff.** The moment the question is answered, shown on screen and not only said. Leave room after it: a beat of silence or a reaction.
- **Open loops.** Raise a question, park it, and answer it later. Close every loop you open; an unanswered one feels like a mistake.

## Openings that work
- A result: "This is the part that took me three tries."
- A failure shown, not described.
- A claim the viewer will want to test ("Most people set this up backwards").
- A question the viewer already has.
A greeting, a channel intro or a thanks for watching is not an opening. It can come after the hook.

## What to cut or move
- A passage that explains something the picture already shows.
- A second example that makes the same point as the first.
- A tangent that does not serve the question, however good it is. Keep the best one as a separate short.
- A section that is only needed because an earlier one was unclear: fix the earlier one.
- Anything said twice in the same words: keep the better take.

## What to keep even when it was not planned
- A real laugh, a mistake the host owns, a reaction to something that did not work, a line said better off the cuff than in the script.
- An honest opinion. These are what viewers remember and what separates a person from a presenter.

## Changing the order
Change the order only when a first-time viewer would follow the new order better: the term is explained before it is used, the setback comes before the fix, the payoff is not given away early. If the recorded order already works, leave it.
