# Takes and splicing

A host often says the same thing more than once: a restart after a stumble, a second attempt that is better, a line repeated in another recording. These rules say which one the cut plays.

## Choosing between takes
- The later take usually wins: people improve as they repeat. Choose the earlier one only when the later one stops short, stumbles, or loses energy.
- Prefer the take that finishes the thought and lands the last words cleanly. A great start with a trailing-off end loses to a plain take that ends well.
- Prefer the take with the better delivery over the one with fewer pauses. Pauses are cheap to cut; flat delivery cannot be fixed.
- A take with a mispronounced or wrong word is out, unless the same words are fixed by taking one sentence from another take.
- When two takes say different things, they are not takes of the same line: keep both, or ask which one the video needs.

## Splicing pieces of two takes
- Join at a sentence boundary, never in the middle of a word or a breath. Both sides need a natural pause or a visible cut-away.
- A spliced piece needs at least the number of words in `splice_min_words` from the other take, so the cut does not flicker between fragments.
- When the voice, the framing or the lighting changes at the join, hide it: a cut-away, a graphic or a punch-in over the join.
- Do not splice across a change of subject, a laugh or a deliberate pause. Those belong to one take.

## When in doubt
Keep both takes in the cut as options and let the host choose. A wrong automatic choice costs more than one extra click.
